This one holds yours.

Chapter One

Two Skies

Before there was an us, there were two lights that had never heard each other’s names.

One, over Maple Street, stayed on too late — reading past the sensible hour, always one more chapter.

The other, across the river, kept guessing the endings of films out loud — to an apartment that never answered back.

Nine miles apart. A thousand near-misses. The same rain on both their windows.

They did not know it yet, but the sky was already rearranging.

Chapter Two

Perihelion

per·i·he·li·on — the point at which two bodies are closest

October 14, 2015

A bookshop with a leaking roof. The rain came down like it had somewhere to be.

Neither of them did.

She was holding the last copy. He was holding the same book — older, annotated, falling apart at the spine.

“Mine has notes,” he said. It wasn’t a great line. It didn’t need to be.

The sky warmed that night. It never fully cooled again.

Chapter Four

Weather

No sky stays clear for ten years.

There was the year the phone kept ringing with bad news. The winter that would not end. Months when the money was thin and the patience thinner.

The lights dimmed.

They did not go out.

Look — you can still see them. Holding.

Some nights were nothing at all. A lamp. A book. Two people breathing in the same quiet.

Those were the best ones.

Chapter Five

Tonight

Point at ten years of nights, and this is what you’d draw.

Not a straight line. A constellation.
